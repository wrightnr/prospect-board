RB Prospect Board

College production model for dynasty running backs · updated Nov 13, 2026

What this is

A dynasty prospect model for quarterbacks, running backs, wide receivers and tight ends that grades players from their first college season through the NFL draft, and checks every call against what actually happened in the NFL. College and NFL stats update automatically every Tuesday.

The process

  1. Career Score — a college production grade built season by season, accounting for when in a player’s career the production came and the level of competition. This is the score that drives the Devy Tracker.
  2. Pre Draft — adds a film evaluation to the production grade once a player declares.
  3. Final Grade — adds draft capital after the draft (or a projected pick before it).
  4. Projected PPG and chances — turns the Final Grade into an expected PPR points per game over NFL years 1–3, and the odds of reaching three tiers: RB1 or WR1 (about a top-12 finish), RB2 or WR2 (about top 24), and Flex or WR3 (about top 36). Running back tiers use PPG averages; receiver, tight end and quarterback tiers use the best single-season finish.

On the Devy Tracker, Pctl vs Drafted compares a player with drafted players at the same point in their college careers, and Band groups that percentile.

Checking the calls

Model Call is the model’s pre-draft read: Likely hit, Toss-up or Long shot to become a starter-level dynasty asset on his rookie deal. Running backs are judged on their average over NFL years 1–3 (12+ PPR points per game, about an RB24), since they produce right away. Receivers, tight ends and quarterbacks are judged on their best season in years 1–3 (a top-24 WR, top-12 TE or top-12 QB finish), since most need time to break out or earn the job. Once three NFL seasons are complete, the Verdict compares that call with reality: Hit called, Correct fade, Bust, Missed sleeper, or a toss-up result.

Year 2–3 Outlook updates the call after a player’s rookie season, combining the Final Grade with what he did as a rookie: Locked in, Regression risk, Breakout candidate, Watch or Fading. A strong pre-draft grade after a quiet rookie year is the classic breakout setup; a rookie hit the model never liked is the classic sell-high.

Rookie Draft

The Rookie Draft tab turns the model into a draft order for every class since 2016, with all four positions on one board, in superflex or 1QB. Each class is checked against the FantasyPros expert consensus taken right after the NFL draft, and, once three NFL seasons are in, against what the players actually produced.

How accurate is it?

R-squared runs from 0 to 1 and measures how much of the spread in NFL Y1–3 PPG each phase explains. Each step adds accuracy on top of the last. Results on future draft classes will likely run a little lower than these historical numbers, and NFL outcomes also depend on injuries, landing spot and opportunity, which no pre-draft model can see. When you filter to a small group, check how many players are in it before trusting a hit rate.

How to read this board
Pick
Where we’d take the player in your rookie draft (set rounds and league size above). Our board combines our prospect model with the expert consensus.
Pos
Position and rank at that position on our board (WR2 = second WR we’d take).
NFL Pick
Overall pick in the NFL draft. Before the draft, a projection.
Consensus
Where FantasyPros experts ranked the player within this class right after the NFL draft, shown as a draft slot. For the newest class during draft season (April–August) it’s their latest ranking.
vs Consensus
How far our board is from the experts: ▲3 = we’re 3 spots higher on him, ▼3 = 3 spots lower, = means we agree.
Consensus Now
His rank within the class in today’s FantasyPros dynasty rankings (updated weekly), so you can see how opinions have moved since the draft.
NFL Result
His rank in the class for actual NFL fantasy value: PPR points scored above a starter-level line at his position (QB24 in superflex, QB12 in 1QB, RB30, WR42, TE14), added up over NFL years 1–3. Green = top 12 (first-round value), amber = 13–24, gray = later; – = no starter-level season yet. Classes still in their first three years show results so far.
