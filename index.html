RB Prospect Board

College production model for dynasty running backs · updated Nov 25, 2026

What this is

A dynasty prospect model for quarterbacks, running backs, wide receivers and tight ends that grades players from their first college season through the NFL draft, and checks every call against what actually happened in the NFL. College and NFL stats update automatically every Tuesday.

The process

  1. Career Score — a college production grade built season by season, accounting for when in a player’s career the production came and the level of competition. This is the score that drives the Devy Tracker.
  2. Pre Draft — adds a film evaluation to the production grade once a player declares.
  3. Final Grade — adds draft capital after the draft (or a projected pick before it).
  4. Projected PPG and chances — turns the Final Grade into an expected PPR points per game over NFL years 1–3, and the odds of reaching three tiers: RB1 or WR1 (about a top-12 finish), RB2 or WR2 (about top 24), and Flex or WR3 (about top 36). Tight ends and quarterbacks use elite, TE1/QB1 and TE2/QB2 tiers (top 6, 12 and 18 for tight ends; top 6, 12 and 24 for quarterbacks). Running back tiers use PPG averages; receiver, tight end and quarterback tiers use the best single-season finish.

On the Devy Tracker, Pctl vs Drafted compares a player with drafted players at the same point in their college careers, and Band groups that percentile.

Checking the calls

Model Call is the model’s pre-draft read: Likely hit, Toss-up or Long shot to become a starter-level dynasty asset on his rookie deal. Running backs are judged on their average over NFL years 1–3 (12+ PPR points per game, about an RB24), since they produce right away. Receivers, tight ends and quarterbacks are judged on their best season in years 1–3 (a top-24 WR, top-12 TE or top-12 QB finish), since most need time to break out or earn the job. Once three NFL seasons are complete, the Verdict compares that call with reality: Hit called, Correct fade, Bust, Missed sleeper, or a toss-up result.

Year 2–3 Outlook updates the call after a player’s rookie season, combining the Final Grade with what he did as a rookie: Locked in, Regression risk, Breakout candidate, Watch or Fading. A strong pre-draft grade after a quiet rookie year is the classic breakout setup; a rookie hit the model never liked is the classic sell-high.

Rookie Draft

The Rookie Draft tab turns the model into a draft order for every class since 2016, with all four positions on one board, in superflex or 1QB. Each class is checked against the FantasyPros expert consensus taken right after the NFL draft, and, once three NFL seasons are in, against what the players actually produced. For running backs, receivers and tight ends the board leans on the experts, who tested slightly better than our model alone at those positions; for quarterbacks it leans on our model, which tested better. So most picks match the consensus by design, and the gaps are where our model disagrees strongly.

Value Board

The Value Board covers the top ~400 dynasty players in the NFL. It sets each player’s KeepTradeCut price against what his production says he should cost, and against what the market pays for players of a similar age and production. It updates every Tuesday.

It makes two calls. Buy: a young running back, receiver or tight end already producing near the top of the league whose price hasn’t caught up. Sell: a receiver aged 29 or older, past the age cliff. In a backtest on real KeepTradeCut prices from 2022 to 2025, 80% of Buys beat similar players over the next year and 81% of Sells trailed them. Every call is logged and scored, so the live record builds up week by week. Experimental signals are patterns with less evidence behind them, shown separately and tracked the same way.

How accurate is it?

R-squared runs from 0 to 1 and measures how much of the spread in NFL Y1–3 PPG each phase explains. Each step adds accuracy on top of the last. Results on future draft classes will likely run a little lower than these historical numbers, and NFL outcomes also depend on injuries, landing spot and opportunity, which no pre-draft model can see. When you filter to a small group, check how many players are in it before trusting a hit rate.

How the calls work, and how they’ve done

Backtest 2022–2025: Buys 80% right, WR Sells 81%. Tap for the rules, hit rates and what to do.
What counts as a hit: over the 12 months after the call, the player’s KeepTradeCut value did better (buys) or worse (sells) than the median of the players you could have traded for instead: any position, priced within 20% of him on the day of the call (the 8 closest if fewer). No target percentage, and beating them by 1% counts the same as by 40%. A coin flip scores 50%; players with no call landed at 50%. Comparing only within his position instead gives 65–74% overall, so the result doesn’t hinge on that choice.
